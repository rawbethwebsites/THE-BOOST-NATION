    The goal isn't a reply from the CEO

    The goal is to cut through the noise and get your email to the right person. A forward from the top skips the thousands of applications nobody is reading. The person you email doesn't need to have the job for you. They just need to know who does.

    1. Ask for the forward

      Always end with: If you're not the right person, I'd love it if you could connect me with them.

    2. Ask for advice, not a job

      Don't write "please hire me". Ask for guidance on how to best position yourself for the role. People love being a mentor. They hate being put on the spot.

    3. Be specific

      Say exactly what you want: a legal analyst, legal assistant, or paralegal role. The more specific you are, the easier you are to forward.

    Who to email

    Go as high as makes sense: the CEO, a founder, or the head of the function you want to join (chief talent officer, general counsel, head of engineering). Find them on the company site or LinkedIn.

    Finding the address

    Most companies use one pattern for everyone: first.last@, first@ or flast@. Check the pattern on a press release or a colleague's public email, then apply it.

    After you send

    Log it in the tracker. If you hear nothing in a week, send one short, polite follow-up in the same thread. When someone forwards you, thank them and move fast with the new contact.

    Now go email someone you think is out of your league.
